Release notes since A3 (Oct 13)

The game

  • Start: menu over the live city with a how-to-play card. Mode (Dispatcher or Sandbox), shift (AM rush, midday, PM rush, night; 2 sim hours, played at 20×, about 6 real minutes) and difficulty. The best score for that choice is shown. Optional: move one station before the shift, with the coverage map previewing the result.
  • Active: calls (medical, fire, crime, crash) come in at real addresses with a pop-up and a chime; off-screen calls get an arrow at the screen edge. Select a call, pick a unit from the list (ETA, fastest marked) or press Enter, and the unit drives there through traffic with its siren on. Units driving back can be sent again. Random accidents block a lane and bring crash calls, more at rush hour. Calls can also be placed by hand.
  • Sandbox: free play like the A3 build, with no score or call list. Calls and accidents still bring units, sent automatically; place your own accidents, closures and calls and watch the response.
  • End: the shift ends on time (win) or after too many missed calls (loss). The end screen shows a grade, score, calls handled and missed, mean response, a chart of each call's response time against its target, and the best score. Play again or return to the menu.
  • Difficulty: Easy / Normal / Hard set calls per hour (12 / 20 / 30), missed calls allowed (4 / 3 / 2), units (Easy +1 ambulance per hospital, Hard −1 police car per station) and accident rate.

Simulation and pillars

  • Swarm and boids: cars ahead of a unit pull onto the shoulder, the unit passes, they merge back; junctions ahead are held for it. A boids debug view (B) draws each unit's yield reach, held junctions and steering arrows. With yielding a unit crossed a test route in 120 s against 265 s stuck behind the same cars.
  • Pathfinding, made visible: a coverage map (C) shades the city by how fast the nearest free unit could get there on live traffic (multi-source shortest paths). Unit ETAs now account for the siren: units arrive in a median 1.00 of their ETA at the morning peak.
  • City: every building, park and car park is an address with its own entrance; trips run door to door, turning left on gaps in oncoming traffic. Accidents and lane closures block one lane; cars pass one side at a time. Unit and station models, drawn entrances, filler between buildings.
  • Also: ride along with any unit; siren and UI sounds with mute; shortcuts with a help overlay; an error panel instead of a frozen page. From A3 feedback: the OD matrix as 9 districts with live counts, and incidents on any street with rerouting.
  • Stability: 63 automated tests (Vitest): yielding, pre-emption, coverage against A*, unit ETAs against real trips, the call lifecycle, scoring, random accidents, and the morning peak (about 700 cars) with no gridlock.

Known issues

  • One lane per direction. Making way for a siren is drawn as a 2 m move onto the shoulder; units pass only cars that have pulled over.
  • Lane closures are slow. One side goes at a time, so a 65 m closure carries about 650–700 cars an hour; on a busy arterial at the peak, queues build. Cars reroute when the incident is placed, not while they wait.
  • A unit sent while driving back turns round at its next junction, so its first few hundred metres can go the wrong way. Coverage counts it from that junction.
  • Starting a shift reloads the page at the shift's hour with half an hour of traffic run first. A station move is kept; incidents placed before are not.
  • Station placement is one move per shift, onto a building or car park on a block laid out lot by lot.
  • At 300× the browser drops sim time when it falls behind (a step takes about 0.5 ms at the peak).
  • Best scores are kept in this browser only. Call pop-ups last 6 real seconds whatever the speed; at 300× only the newest 3 show (every call still has a pin and a list entry).
  • The gridlock guard (removes a car stopped for 3 minutes) stays as a last resort; it removed none in the tests or in a sweep of 5 scenarios with 24 units each.
  • After an error the page has to be reloaded.
