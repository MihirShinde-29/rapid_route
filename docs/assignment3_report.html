What was implemented

The approved traffic slice runs as a TypeScript + Vite + Three.js browser simulation. Zone density drives a gravity OD matrix and peak-hour trip generation. Vehicles use IDM car-following and junction right-of-way rules. Arterial and downtown junctions have fixed-time signals; other junctions use stop signs. Each trip picks its route at departure with A* over live link travel times, so a longer but less congested route can win.

City and traffic model

  • City: 26 × 22 blocks, a 16 × 12 downtown core with suburbs around it. Arterials every 4th street (2 lanes, 60 km/h), local streets 40 km/h. Blocks vary in size and suburban streets are slightly crooked, with cul-de-sacs, T-junctions, a central park, two smaller parks and a shopping centre.
  • Signals: two-phase fixed timing, 60 s cycle (32 s / 18 s arterial and local green, or 25 s / 25 s), 3 s yellow, 2 s all-red, with offsets that form a green wave along arterials. A car enters on yellow only if it is moving and cannot stop.
  • Trips: cars pull out of and into driveways in the middle of the block, not at junctions.
  • View: brake lights, turn signals, a chase camera that follows the probe car (drag to look around), and speeds from 1× to 300×.

Limits found: at 14,000 trips/h the AM peak gridlocks, so 13,000 is the stable setting. Routes are fixed at departure; cars already driving do not reroute, which is why the probe's actual time is longer than its estimate. Signals do not react to queues.

AI utility

AI coding tools sped up the data structures, routing, rendering, tests and documentation. We reviewed every change, tuned demand and signal timing by running the simulation, and checked results against the tests. No LLM or network API runs in the simulation; it uses classical algorithms only.

Demo and next steps

The video shows AM demand and the OD matrix, the busy city at 60× with zero gridlock removals, signals and stop signs at work, and the probe trip taking the longer, faster route (the Jam short route button makes this reproducible; it also happens on its own at the morning peak).
